1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
======================================================================

1.1 PROJECT PURPOSE

Heloix intends to develop a comprehensive corporate travel, expense, and
spend management platform inspired by the functional capabilities of Perk.

The platform will allow companies to manage employee travel, corporate
travel policies, bookings, approvals, expenses, invoices, reporting,
supplier integrations, and administrative operations through a unified
digital platform.

The product will operate as a multi-tenant SaaS platform, allowing
multiple businesses to register, configure their organizations, invite
employees, manage travel policies, and monitor spending independently.

The platform must support a future expansion into a B2C travel booking
business without requiring a complete architectural redesign.


1.2 PRODUCT VISION

Build a centralized business travel and expense management platform
that simplifies the entire corporate travel lifecycle:

Employee travel request
        |
        v
Policy validation
        |
        v
Manager approval, if required
        |
        v
Travel search and booking
        |
        v
Ticket and reservation management
        |
        v
Trip modifications and cancellations
        |
        v
Expense collection and reconciliation
        |
        v
Financial reporting and accounting integration


1.3 CORE BUSINESS OBJECTIVES

BO-01: Enable businesses to manage employee travel through one platform.

BO-02: Provide access to bookable flights, hotels, trains, rental cars,
       and other supported travel services.

BO-03: Automate travel approval and policy enforcement.

BO-04: Centralize booking records, travel documents, and invoices.

BO-05: Simplify expense submission, review, reimbursement tracking,
       and financial reconciliation.

BO-06: Provide administrators with spending controls and organizational
       visibility.

BO-07: Enable integrations with external travel suppliers, expense
       systems, HR systems, and accounting platforms.

BO-08: Generate revenue through SaaS subscriptions, transaction fees,
       supplier commissions, negotiated service fees, or combinations
       of these models.

BO-09: Support international customers, multiple currencies, multiple
       languages, and country-specific configuration.

BO-10: Establish a scalable foundation for future B2C travel services,
       advanced automation, and AI-assisted operations.

1.5 TERMINOLOGY

Organization:
A business subscribing to Voyentra.

Tenant:
An isolated customer environment within the SaaS platform.

Traveler:
An employee or authorized individual who can book or request travel.

Travel Request:
A request for authorization to undertake a trip.

Travel Booking:
A confirmed reservation with a travel supplier.

Travel Inventory:
The collection of flights, hotels, rental cars, and other travel
products available for search and booking.

Travel Supplier:
An airline, hotel, global distribution system, travel technology
provider, online travel agency, or other booking provider.

Travel Policy:
A collection of rules governing permitted corporate travel.

Approver:
A person authorized to approve or reject a travel request or expense.

Travel Administrator:
A customer-side user responsible for managing organizational travel.

Platform Administrator:
A Heloix employee authorized to manage the SaaS platform.

Expense:
A business-related expenditure submitted for review or reimbursement.

Multi-Tenancy:
An architecture in which multiple customer organizations use the same
platform while their data and permissions remain isolated.

11. TRAVEL BOOKING AND CHECKOUT
======================================================================

11.1 BOOKING WORKFLOW

The booking process shall include:

1. Search inventory.
2. Select an available option.
3. Review itinerary or accommodation details.
4. Validate traveler information.
5. Evaluate travel policy.
6. Obtain required approval.
7. Revalidate price and availability.
8. Select permitted payment method.
9. Submit the booking request.
10. Process the supplier response.
11. Record the confirmed booking.
12. Store supplier references.
13. Generate or retrieve travel documents.
14. Notify the traveler and relevant administrators.

A modular monolith is a reasonable initial architecture if the
engineering team is small and traffic is not yet established.

A distributed microservices architecture should be introduced only
where scaling, independent deployment, fault isolation, or organizational
requirements justify its additional complexity.

The system should preserve clear module boundaries so that individual
components can be extracted later if necessary.

- Use stable identifiers for core records.

36. KEY RISKS AND DEPENDENCIES
======================================================================

RISK-01: TRAVEL INVENTORY ACCESS

Description:
Travel booking functionality depends on access to suitable suppliers.

Impact:
Without usable inventory APIs and commercial agreements, the platform
cannot deliver a complete live-booking experience.

Mitigation:
Select and validate suppliers before committing to the full booking
implementation.


RISK-02: BOOKING RELIABILITY

Description:
Supplier timeouts, duplicate requests, and asynchronous confirmations
can result in uncertain booking states.

Impact:
Potential duplicate reservations, financial discrepancies, and
customer support incidents.

Mitigation:
Implement idempotency, reconciliation, state management, and supplier
reference tracking.


RISK-03: PAYMENT COMPLEXITY

Description:
Payment authorization, supplier settlement, refunds, and currencies
vary across providers and jurisdictions.

Impact:
Financial loss, failed bookings, and reconciliation problems.

Mitigation:
Choose a suitable payment model and provider before finalizing
transaction architecture.


RISK-04: REFUND AND CANCELLATION HANDLING

Description:
Supplier cancellation and refund processes are not always synchronous.

Impact:
Inaccurate booking status and incorrect customer expectations.

Mitigation:
Track cancellation and refund as separate workflows.


RISK-05: SCOPE EXPANSION

Description:
Travel booking, expenses, card management, accounting, AI, and B2C
represent several substantial product domains.

Impact:
Cost overruns, delayed delivery, and unstable software.

Mitigation:
Prioritize product capabilities according to business value and
technical dependencies.


RISK-06: INTERNATIONAL COMPLIANCE

Description:
The platform may process employee, passport, financial, and travel data
across jurisdictions.

Impact:
Regulatory exposure and contractual restrictions.

Mitigation:
Complete privacy, legal, and data-residency assessments before
operating in each target market.


RISK-07: CORPORATE CARD DEPENDENCIES

Description:
Card issuing and transaction access require appropriate providers.

Impact:
Card functionality cannot be delivered solely through application
development.

Mitigation:
Integrate authorized financial providers and clearly separate
transaction tracking from actual money movement.


RISK-08: SUPPORT OPERATIONS

Description:
Travel changes and disruptions may require timely human intervention.

Impact:
High support costs and customer dissatisfaction.

Mitigation:
Define supplier support arrangements, escalation procedures, staffing,
and service-level commitments.


RISK-09: PRICING AND UNIT ECONOMICS

Description:
Supplier fees, support costs, payment charges, and transaction margins
may vary significantly.

Impact:
A high-volume business may still be unprofitable.

Mitigation:
Model contribution margin by booking type, geography, customer segment,
and subscription plan before finalizing commercial pricing.


RISK-10: PRODUCT PARITY

Description:
A broad feature checklist does not guarantee equivalent operational
quality to an established platform.

Impact:
The platform may look complete while failing under real booking,
refund, reconciliation, or support scenarios.

Mitigation:
Prioritize end-to-end reliability and operational readiness, not just
the number of implemented screens.
